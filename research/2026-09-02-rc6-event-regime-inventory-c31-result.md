The slow-tip travel is recomputed over the accepted 24-frame liquid window,
not copied from the full 120-frame motor-screen maximum. The one-voxel boundary
is a sampling descriptor; it is not a learned stability threshold, and
integer-frame endpoints do not bound unmeasured intra-frame motion.

Three findings change the next action:

1. There is no accepted high-speed liquid recipe in this evidence. Width 3
   improves fragmentation but still fails both volume checks. The future
   automatic recipe catalog must remain empty for that regime.
2. Slow-tip versus impact is not an isolated speed experiment. The bound
   configuration also differs in effector samples, fractional threshold,
   timestep ceiling, domain placement and window. C29 is a controlled
   bandwidth intervention *within impact*, not enough evidence for a
   speed-to-bandwidth mapping across scenes.
3. The existing pure policy permits at most 24 PREVIEW frames. The 36-frame
   impact is a separately authorized research experiment, not an already
   integrated product request. Keep that limit; a future event-window adapter
   needs its own versioned proof, not a silent limit increase.

The frozen design separates sampling context, full physical acceptance and
cache/quality admission. Any failed physical check retains rejection, even if
another metric improves. New geometry, relation topology, motion envelope,
tier or window remains uncalibrated until independently validated. Exact
accepted physics alone still does not establish visual or film quality.
